1 · Overall performance

Sales grew 51% from 2015 to 2018, but margin peaked in 2017 and slipped in 2018.

  • Sales: $484K (2015) → $733K (2018); 2016 dipped -2.8%.
  • Average order value fell from $500 to $435 while orders rose 74%: growth is more, smaller orders.
  • Central has the lowest margin (7.9%) and the heaviest discounting (24% average).

2 · Product sub-categories

A few high-growth, high-margin lines carry the business; three sub-categories lose money.

  • Tables lose $17.7K on $207K sales (-8.6% margin); Bookcases and Supplies also negative.
  • Copiers: +80% sales CAGR with a 37% margin. Machines: shrinking (-11% CAGR) and loss-making in 2018.
  • 55% of Tables lines are discounted by more than 20%.

3 · Promotion effectiveness

Discounts above 20% cost margin and do not buy volume.

  • Margin is 29.5% at no discount, 11.6% at 11-20%, and negative above 20%.
  • Average quantity per line is ~3.7-3.9 in every band: deeper discounts do not coincide with bigger baskets.
  • Lines above 20% discount are 14% of volume but erase $135K (32%) of the profit earned at ≤20%.
  • Losses on loss-making lines rose from $32K (2015) to $54K (2018) at a flat ~15.6% average discount.

4 · Customer behaviour & growth

Growth comes from existing customers; acquisition has almost stopped.

  • New customers per year: 136 (2016) → 51 (2017) → 11 (2018). 2015 is the first year in the data, so all its customers count as new.
  • Retention improved from 73% to 87%; 98% of customers order at least twice.
  • Top 20% of customers generate 48% of sales; RFM Champions are 24% of customers and 40% of sales.

5 · Recommendations

  1. Cap discounts at 20% with manager approval above that threshold.
  2. Re-price or restructure Tables, Bookcases and Supplies.
  3. Review the Central region's discount policy.
  4. Invest in Copiers, Accessories and Appliances; bundle high-margin Paper, Labels and Envelopes.
  5. Restart customer acquisition; run win-back for At Risk and a loyalty tier for Champions.
Discount is used as a proxy for promotions (no campaign data in the source). Findings show association, not causation. Source: Superstore sample dataset.
